How I Work

Most people design for a hypothetical user. I design for a specific person in a specific moment — the AV operator scrambling between songs on a Sunday morning, the indie artist trying to be found by the right 500 people, the business owner who can't afford a full team but still needs to show up professionally.

The details that make something work — the micro-copy, the load state, the fallback — those aren't polish. They're the product.

AI doesn't change that. It just raises the ceiling on what one person can build alone.

About

I do the design,
the build,
and the launch.

I graduated from Humber College's Multimedia Design and Development program in 2021 and have been in market ever since — working full-time and freelancing simultaneously, which means I've had to think commercially about design from day one.

My background is genuinely cross-disciplinary. I've done digital operations, social strategy, motion graphics, branding, web development, and audio production — not as scattered side interests, but as a single integrated practice. I understand how a brand sounds, moves, looks, and converts.

What shifted my work in recent years was AI — not as a shortcut, but as a design material. I started integrating Claude and generative tools directly into the products I build, which opened up a new category of work: experiences that are interactive, adaptive, and genuinely intelligent.

I'm based in Markham, Ontario. I work best when the problem is specific, the audience is real, and the stakes are high enough to matter.

Outside of screens, I produce music — which probably explains why I care as much about the invisible parts of a product as the visible ones.
